03 / Residential strata

Residential strata management

We manage a diverse range of properties: residential apartment blocks, townhouse complexes and villas. Competent management is not an administrative nicety in a residential scheme, it is the difference between a building people want to live in and one they want to sell out of.

Request a quote

01 / How we manage

Managed properly, reported clearly

All of our residential schemes are managed with a comprehensive online reporting package that keeps the committee informed at all times. Levies, expenditure, work orders and records sit in one place, and your manager brings anything that needs a decision to you before it becomes urgent.

02 / Included

What residential schemes get

  • A named manager who knows your building
  • Levy notices issued on schedule and arrears followed up
  • Quarterly statements written to be read
  • Annual general meeting run properly, with minutes distributed
  • Maintenance quoted, engaged, tracked and closed out
  • Insurance valued, renewed and claimed on your behalf
  • By-law questions answered without a legal bill
  • After hours emergency line for residents
  • Portal access for every owner, 24/7

03 / Common questions

Common questions for residential committees

Read all the FAQs

The owners corporation, funded through levies, under the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015. Common property generally covers the structure and the shared parts of the building: external walls, the roof, common area floors, lifts, and shared services. Anything inside your lot is generally yours, unless a registered by-law says otherwise.

Where responsibility is not obvious, we check the strata plan and the registered by-laws rather than guess, and we tell you which it is before any work is arranged.

What counts as common property

Because the costs behind them do. Insurance premiums have risen sharply across NSW, contractor rates have followed, and buildings get older rather than younger, which means more maintenance and larger capital works.

A levy that never rises is usually a capital works fund quietly running out. We would rather show your committee the ten year position and let owners decide with real numbers in front of them.

The owners corporation can charge interest and take steps to recover the debt. Under the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015, overdue levies can attract simple interest, and continued arrears can be recovered through the courts, together with reasonable recovery costs. An owner in arrears also generally cannot vote at general meetings. Under our fee structure, the cost of an overdue levy notice or recovery instructions is paid by the owner concerned, not the rest of the building. If you are having trouble paying, talk to your manager early.
