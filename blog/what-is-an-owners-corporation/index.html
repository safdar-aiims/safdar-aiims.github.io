What Is an Owners Corporation, and What Do They Do?

20 April 20265 min readBy Strata United

If you own property in a strata scheme, chances are you’ve come across the term owners corporation, but what it actually does isn’t always clear.

One moment you’re reviewing levies. Next, there’s talk of maintenance approvals or compliance obligations. Responsibility can feel blurred, especially in larger buildings across Sydney and Parramatta.

So, what is an owners corporation, and why does it play such a central role in strata living?

This guide breaks it down in plain terms: what it is, how it operates, and why effective owners corporation management matters for long-term property value and stability.

What Is an Owners Corporation?

An owners corporation is the legal entity formed when a strata plan is registered. It includes all lot owners within the scheme.

In simple terms, it is responsible for managing and maintaining the shared areas of the property, often referred to as common property.

If you’ve ever asked, What is an owners corporation, think of it as the body that ensures the building functions as a whole. Not just physically, but financially and legally as well.

Its authority comes from strata legislation, including the Owners Corporation Act, which outlines how it must operate and what duties it must fulfil.

The Legal Framework Behind Owners Corporations

Every owners' corporation operates within a defined legal structure. This ensures consistency, accountability, and protection for all stakeholders.

In Australia, this is governed through legislation such as the Owners Corporations Act and, in some jurisdictions, the Owners Corporations Act 2006.

For New South Wales, strata schemes follow state-specific laws. If you need a deeper look at how these rules apply locally, this guide on NSW strata legislation explains the framework in more detail.

These laws cover:

  • how decisions are made;
  • financial responsibilities;
  • dispute resolution processes;
  • maintenance obligations;
  • compliance requirements.

They set the baseline. But how effectively those rules are applied often depends on the quality of management.

What Does an Owners Corporation Do?

The role of an owners' corporation goes well beyond basic upkeep. It sits at the centre of how a strata scheme operates day to day.

Property Maintenance

The owners' corporation is responsible for maintaining common property. This includes:

  • external walls;
  • roofs;
  • hallways and lifts;
  • shared gardens and driveways.

Maintenance isn’t optional. It’s a legal obligation. Delays or neglect can lead to larger repair costs and disputes between owners.

Financial Management

Funds are collected through strata levies, which cover both day-to-day expenses and long-term capital works.

If you’re unsure how these contributions are calculated, this breakdown of
strata levies explain where your money goes.

The owners' corporation must:

  • prepare budgets;
  • manage sinking funds;
  • ensure financial transparency.

Poor financial oversight can quickly affect the building’s condition and value.

Compliance and Legal Duties

Compliance sits at the core of the Owners' Corporation Act.

The owners' corporation must ensure the building meets safety standards, including:

  • fire safety compliance;
  • building regulations;
  • insurance obligations.

It also enforces bylaws, which govern how residents use the property.

For clarity around responsibilities within a scheme, this guide on who is responsible in strata is a useful reference.

Insurance Responsibilities

Insurance is not optional. The owners' corporation must arrange cover for the building structure and common areas.

This protects all owners collectively, reducing risk exposure in the event of damage or unforeseen incidents.

Who Makes Decisions in an Owners Corporation?

While the owners' corporation includes all lot owners, decisions are not made individually.

Instead, a strata committee is elected to act on behalf of the group.

This committee handles:

  • routine decisions;
  • contractor approvals;
  • ongoing management matters.

Major decisions, such as large expenditures or by-law changes, are voted on during general meetings.

This structure allows the scheme to function efficiently, while still giving owners a voice.

Why Owners Corporation Management Matters

Running an owners corporation is not a passive role. It involves constant oversight, coordination, and compliance.

This is where professional owners' corporation management becomes valuable.

Without it, common issues begin to surface:

  • inconsistent maintenance;
  • delayed decision-making;
  • financial mismanagement;
  • unclear communication between owners.

A structured approach ensures these risks are controlled.

Experienced providers like Strata United offer tailored strata management services that support committees and streamline operations.

The result is simple. Fewer disputes. Better financial control. A building that holds its value.

Common Challenges Owners' Corporations Face

Even well-run schemes encounter challenges. The difference lies in how they are managed.

Typical issues include:

  • disagreements between owners;
  • unclear responsibility for repairs;
  • budget shortfalls;
  • compliance gaps.

In growing areas like Sydney and Parramatta, these challenges can escalate quickly as buildings become larger and more complex.

Access to clear processes and the right support makes all the difference.

When Should You Consider Professional Strata Management?

Not every scheme starts with professional support. But there are clear signs when it becomes necessary.

You may need external owners corporation management if:

  • meetings are unproductive or inconsistent;
  • maintenance issues are recurring;
  • financial records lack clarity;
  • compliance obligations feel unclear.

Switching providers is more straightforward than many assume. If your current setup isn’t working, you can switch strata manager with minimal disruption.

Final Thoughts

An owners corporation sits at the centre of every strata scheme. It keeps the building running, protects shared assets, and ensures compliance with the law.

When managed well, it creates stability. When overlooked, problems tend to surface quickly.

For property owners and investors across Sydney and Parramatta, clarity is essential. So is the right support.

FAQs About Owners Corporations

What is an owners' corporation responsible for?

An owners corporation manages common property, finances, compliance, and insurance for the strata scheme.

Is an owners corporation the same as a body corporate?

Yes. In many states, the terms are used interchangeably, although terminology may vary depending on legislation.

What does the Owners' Corporation Act cover?

The Owners Corporation Act outlines governance, financial management, maintenance obligations, and dispute resolution processes.

Who pays for repairs in an owners' corporation?

Repairs to common property are funded through levies paid by all owners. Individual lot owners cover repairs within their own property.

Can you change strata management companies?

Yes. If your current provider is not meeting expectations, you can transition to a new one. For guidance, visit the strata FAQs or contact Strata United.
