3:T2545,<p>In strata schemes across Sydney, few issues create more confusion than common property.</p>
<p>Owners often assume responsibility sits elsewhere. Committees hesitate before approving works. Disputes arise over balconies, garages, and even windows.</p>
<p>So, what exactly is common property in strata, and why does it matter?</p>
<p>For Owners Corporations and committees in Sydney and Parramatta, clarity here protects budgets, compliance, and building value.</p>
<h2>What Is Common Property?</h2>
<p>At its simplest, what is common property?</p>
<p>Under NSW strata law, common property refers to all areas of a strata scheme that are not part of an individual lot.</p>
<p>If it is not shown as part of a lot on the registered strata plan, it is generally common property.</p>
<p>That includes shared physical structures. It also includes certain services and infrastructure that support the building as a whole.</p>
<p>In practical terms, most common areas include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Building exteriors</li>
<li>Roofs</li>
<li>Structural walls</li>
<li>Lifts</li>
<li>Foyers</li>
<li>Driveways</li>
<li>Stairwells</li>
<li>Shared gardens</li>
<li>Basement corridors</li>
</ul>
<p>These common areas are owned collectively by the Owners Corporation. Responsibility for repair and maintenance follows that ownership.</p>
<h2>What Is Considered Common Property in Strata?</h2>
<p>The next question committees often ask is: what is considered common property in strata?</p>
<p>The answer depends on the registered strata plan. Generally, the following fall within a common area:</p>
<ul>
<li>External walls</li>
<li>Boundary walls</li>
<li>Structural beams and columns</li>
<li>Common plumbing and wiring</li>
<li>Shared air-conditioning systems</li>
<li>Roof membranes</li>
<li>Fire safety systems</li>
<li>Entry doors (in many schemes)</li>
</ul>
<p>In Sydney apartment buildings, this also often includes balconies, specifically the slab and waterproofing membrane. Tiles or surface finishes may differ. It depends on the plan.</p>
<p>Mixed-use buildings in Parramatta frequently present more complex layouts. Shared service risers, loading docks, and mechanical plant rooms almost always form part of common property strata.</p>
<p>Each scheme must check its strata plan. Assumptions cause problems.</p>
<h2>Is a Garage Common Property in Strata?</h2>
<p>One of the most searched questions is: is a garage common property in strata?</p>
<p>The answer varies.</p>
<p>In many Sydney strata schemes, basement parking spaces are common property with exclusive use rights granted to a lot owner. That means the Owners Corporation owns the structure, including concrete slab and line markings. The lot owner has the right to use the space.</p>
<p>In other schemes, garages may form part of the lot boundary. You must review the registered strata plan and any by-laws granting exclusive use.</p>
<p>Where parking is common property, the Owners Corporation remains responsible for structural maintenance. Surface damage caused by a lot owner may still fall back to that owner.</p>
<p>Clarity avoids disputes.</p>
<h2>Grey Areas That Cause Confusion</h2>
<p>Strata disputes rarely arise over foyers or lifts. They arise over boundaries.</p>
<p>Here are the areas that often cause uncertainty in common property strata schemes:</p>
<h3>Balconies</h3>
<p>Structural slab and waterproofing are usually common property. Surface tiles may be lot owner responsibility.</p>
<h3>Windows</h3>
<p>External window frames are commonly common property. Internal glass panes may vary.</p>
<h3>Pipes</h3>
<p>Shared vertical pipes servicing multiple lots are common property. Internal pipes servicing one lot are often lot responsibility.</p>
<h3>Doors</h3>
<p>Entry doors to lots are frequently common property because they affect fire safety and building appearance.</p>
<h3>Exclusive Use Areas</h3>
<p>Courtyards or parking spaces granted under by-law remain common property, even if one owner uses them exclusively.</p>
<p>Each Sydney building may differ. The strata plan always governs.</p>
<h2>Who Maintains Common Property?</h2>
<p>The Owners Corporation carries the legal duty to repair and maintain common property.</p>
<p>That obligation is ongoing. It is not discretionary.</p>
<p>In practice, this means:</p>
<ul>
<li>Scheduling routine inspections</li>
<li>Approving capital works</li>
<li>Budgeting for future replacement</li>
<li>Addressing defects promptly</li>
<li>Coordinating contractors</li>
</ul>
<p>Professional <a href="/strata-management-services">strata management services</a> play a key role here. Experienced managers ensure compliance with NSW requirements and prevent reactive spending.</p>
<p>In growing precincts such as Parramatta, where high-rise developments continue to increase, proactive oversight is critical.</p>
<p>Deferred maintenance increases levies later.</p>
<h2>Why Common Property Matters to Owners Corporations</h2>
<p>Clear identification of common property strata protects the financial position of the scheme.</p>
<p>If responsibility is unclear, costs shift unfairly. That creates conflict. It also delays essential repairs.</p>
<p>Well-managed schemes in Sydney maintain:</p>
<ul>
<li>Updated capital works funds</li>
<li>Accurate strata plans</li>
<li>Clear by-laws</li>
<li>Transparent maintenance records</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common Property in Residential vs Mixed-Use Buildings</h2>
<p>Residential schemes typically focus on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Shared recreational spaces</li>
<li>Pools</li>
<li>Gardens</li>
<li>Lifts</li>
</ul>
<p>Mixed-use buildings in Sydney CBD or Parramatta often include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Retail frontages</li>
<li>Shared loading docks</li>
<li>Plant rooms servicing both residential and commercial lots</li>
<li>Shared car parks</li>
</ul>
<p>The scope of common areas expands significantly in mixed-use developments.</p>
<p>Coordination becomes more complex. Maintenance schedules must reflect commercial operating hours and safety standards. This is where structured strata oversight makes a difference.</p>

<p>Routine programs should cover:</p>
<ul>
<li>Roof inspections</li>
<li>Lift servicing</li>
<li>Fire safety compliance</li>

<h3>Is a garage common property in strata?</h3>
